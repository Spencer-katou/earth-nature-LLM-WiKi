# NOAA — Ocean currents (notes)

- Currents: continuous, directed movement of ocean water at the surface and at depth, local to global. Drivers: winds, water density, tides. Coastlines and seafloor shape them.
- Surface currents: driven by global winds powered by the sun; act in the upper ~100 m; carry heat from the tropics toward the poles.
- Coriolis effect: Earth's rotation deflects winds and water — right in the Northern Hemisphere, left in the Southern.
- Thermohaline circulation: deep currents driven by density (temperature + salinity). In polar regions (e.g., North Atlantic) water cools, and sea-ice formation leaves salt behind → colder, saltier, denser water sinks; surface water flows in to replace it. Deep currents extend thousands of meters down.
- Global conveyor belt: connected deep + surface currents circling the globe; ~1,000-year time span; critical for climate and for nutrient and CO2 cycles.
- Upwelling: brings cold, nutrient-rich deep water to the surface → feeds phytoplankton and seaweed, the base of food webs.
- Gulf Stream: from the tropical Caribbean, along the US East Coast, across the Atlantic toward Europe; carries ~150× the water of the Amazon River; keeps much of Northern Europe warmer than other places at the same latitude.
- Marine life: currents are a "liquid wind" — they deliver food and nutrients and disperse larvae and reproductive cells.
- Measurement: Global Drifter Array, Argo floats (2 million measurements reached in 2018).
- Not covered: gyres, current speeds, other named currents.
