# NOAA — Ocean acidification (notes)

## Chemistry
- Atmospheric CO2 dissolves into seawater; water + CO2 → carbonic acid (H2CO3, weak acid) → hydrogen ions (H+) + bicarbonate (HCO3-).
- Extra H+ bonds with carbonate ions (CO3 2-), leaving fewer for calcifying organisms.
- pH scale 0–14, 7 neutral; more H+ → lower pH → more acidic.

## pH
- Since the industrial revolution (200+ years), surface ocean pH has fallen 0.1 units ≈ 30% increase in acidity (log scale).
- Current average surface pH ~8.1 (still basic).
- Business-as-usual emissions → surface pH could reach ~7.8 by 2100.
- Last time ocean pH was this low: middle Miocene, 14–17 million years ago (Earth several degrees warmer, major extinction underway).
- Affects the whole ocean including estuaries.

## Uptake
- Ocean absorbs about 30% of CO2 released into the atmosphere; uptake increases as atmospheric CO2 rises.

## Effects
- Already affecting oysters and corals (calcium carbonate builders): fewer carbonate ions; at low enough pH shells and skeletons can dissolve.
- Pteropods (pea-sized sea snails, food for krill to whales): shells slowly dissolved after 45 days in seawater with projected 2100 chemistry; severe dissolution already found in the Southern Ocean.
- Clownfish detect predators less well; larvae struggle to find habitat.
- Some algae and seagrass may benefit; seaweed farming studied to slow local acidification (California oyster hatchery, 2019).
- Dungeness crab (US Pacific Northwest, most valuable West Coast fishery): NOAA-funded 2020 study found effects on young crabs' shells and sensory organs.
- Billions of people rely on seafood as primary protein.

## Monitoring
- NOAA Ocean Acidification Program; PMEL carbon data; buoys (Arctic 2013, American Samoa 2019); Saildrone circumnavigation of Antarctica (2019). Sustained global monitoring is only beginning; cascading food-web effects cannot yet be predicted exactly.
