# NOAA — Coral bleaching (notes)

## Mechanism
- Corals host zooxanthellae, their main food source and source of colour. Under stress (changes in temperature, light, nutrients) the coral expels the algae and turns completely white.
- A bleached coral is not dead; it can recover if stress is not severe. Prolonged algae loss + continued stress → death. Bleached corals are more vulnerable to disease.

## Causes (NOAA infographic)
1. Warmer ocean temperature — the leading cause, linked to climate change.
2. Runoff and pollution (storm rain dilutes seawater; pollutants).
3. Overexposure to sunlight in shallow water during high temperatures.
4. Extreme low tides exposing corals to air.
- Cold water can also bleach: January 2010, Florida Keys, water 6.7 °C colder than typical; some coral death.

## Consequences
- 2005: the U.S. lost half of its Caribbean coral reefs in one year; thermal stress that year exceeded the previous 20 years combined (warm water near Virgin Islands / Puerto Rico).

## Global bleaching events (Coral Reef Watch, updated 2026-06-02)
| Event | Years | Reef area under bleaching-level heat stress |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1998 | not stated |
| 2nd | 2010 | not stated |
| 3rd | 2014–2017 | 68.2% |
| 4th | 2023–2025 | ~84.4% (1 Jan 2023 – 30 Sep 2025) |
- 4th event: mass bleaching documented in at least 83 countries and territories; NOAA confirmed it on 2024-04-15; described as the biggest to date; headline says it "likely ended in 2025".

## Monitoring
- NOAA Coral Reef Watch: daily global 5 km satellite heat-stress products; Bleaching Alert Area with Alert Levels 1–5 (Levels 3–5 = extreme heat stress beyond Alert Level 2, can cause multi-species or near-complete reef mortality); 4-month outlook, virtual stations, marine heatwave products.
- Degree Heating Weeks definition and numeric thresholds NOT given on these pages.
