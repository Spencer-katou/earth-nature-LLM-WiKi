# NSIDC — Sea ice & permafrost (notes)

## Sea ice
- Frozen ocean water that forms, grows and melts in the ocean. Icebergs, glaciers, ice shelves float too but originate on land. Usually snow-covered most of the year.
- Albedo: open ocean reflects ~6% of sunlight; sea ice 50–70%; snow-covered sea ice up to 90%. When ice melts, ocean absorbs ~90% → feedback making poles the most climate-sensitive regions.
- Arctic summer sea ice thickness and extent have declined dramatically since the 1980s. Arctic ice matters more for global climate because more survives summer.
- Antarctic sea ice fluctuates more, is thinner and nearly all melts in southern summer. Antarctica (continent surrounded by ocean) is isolated by winds/currents; the Arctic (ocean surrounded by land) is tightly linked to surrounding climate.
- Extent = ocean area with ≥15% ice concentration; area = sum of actual concentration; extent > area. NSIDC reports extent.
- Arctic minimum typically September, maximum March; Antarctic minimum March, maximum September. Arctic minimum occurring later recently; winter recovery weaker.
- New sea ice is salty (brine pockets); brine drains as ice ages; multiyear ice meltwater is fresh enough to drink.
- Satellite record began late 1978 (SMMR, SSM/I, SSMIS); baseline 1981–2010.
- No million-km² extent values, thickness numbers, trend %/decade or record-low years on this page.

## Frozen ground & permafrost
- Frozen ground: soil at or below 0 °C. More than half of Northern Hemisphere land freezes and thaws every year (seasonally frozen ground covers ~50% of NH land).
- Permafrost: soil, sand, sediment or rock at or below 0 °C for at least two years; defined only by temperature. Some is >30% ice.
- Covers ~23 million km², ~25% of exposed NH land; ~65% in Eurasia, ~35% in North America & Greenland; from 84°N (northern Greenland) to 26°N (Himalaya). Also subsea under Arctic shelves; some in Antarctica and high mountains (incl. Qinghai-Tibet Plateau).
- Thickness from <1 m to >1,500 m. Often thousands of years old.
- Active layer: top layer that thaws and refreezes each year (no thickness given).
- Large water bodies moderate temperature → little permafrost in Scandinavia and Iceland; snow cover can insulate and warm the ground.
- Carbon stocks and thaw impacts NOT covered on this page.
