# USGS — Ice, snow, glaciers (notes)

## Definitions
- Snowpack: snow persisting year-round at high elevation/latitude; compressed by its own weight into ice.
- Glacier: large, perennial accumulation of crystalline ice, snow, rock, sediment, often liquid water; originates on land, moves downslope under its own weight and gravity.
- Ice sheet: a glacier covering >50,000 km².

## Water stored in ice
- Page 1 (data: Gleick 1996): ice caps, glaciers, permanent snow ≈ 68.7% of freshwater, ≈ 1.7% of all water; 24,064,000 km³. Total freshwater 35,030,000 km³ (2.5%); total water 1,386,000,000 km³.
- Page 2 FAQ: ~2.1% of all water frozen in glaciers; ~3/4 of freshwater in glaciers. Page 2 bullet: ~69% of freshwater (citing NSIDC).

## Antarctica & Greenland
- Antarctica holds almost 90% of Earth's ice mass. Land under parts of the West Antarctic Ice Sheet is up to 2.5 km below sea level.
- Greenland: ~10% of global ice mass; volume ~2.5 million km³; average thickness ~1,500 m, max ~4,300 m; land pressed into a bowl; up to 517 km³ of ice calves per year (one estimate); icebergs seen as far south as Bermuda.

## Sea level
- All land ice melting → ~70 m (230 ft) rise (some uncertainty).
- Last ice age: sea level ~122 m lower; glaciers covered almost 1/3 of land.
- ~125,000 years ago warm spell: ~5.5 m higher. ~3 million years ago: up to 50 m higher.

## Formation & motion
- Glaciers form where snowfall exceeds summer melt; snow is compressed into ice; flows downhill like "rivers of solid ice", grinding and bulldozing sediment.
- Size: football field to 161 km long. Blue = dense ice absorbing other colors; white = many air bubbles.
- Ice >100,000 years old at the base of some Canadian Arctic ice caps.
- Bering Glacier (Alaska) is North America's longest, 204 km. Kutiah Glacier (Pakistan) surged >12 km in 3 months in 1953 (~112 m/day).

## Role in the water cycle
- Long-term storage, years to centuries; deep ice thousands of years or more. Affects runoff volume, variability, quality.
- Shaped landscapes: Great Lakes, U-shaped Yosemite Valley.
- Ice reflects sunlight, keeps the surface cooler; shrinking ice adds to warming potential.

## Climate & trends
- Last ice age ended ~18,000 years ago (page 1) vs ~20,000 years ago (page 2).
- Alaska: tens of thousands of glaciers, retreating measurably each year. Wolverine Glacier 2012–2019 lost ice equal to a tower >1.2 km tall. Grinnell Glacier (Montana) retreating since the early 1900s.
- Mountain glacier shrinkage attributed to warming since the Little Ice Age ended (late 19th c.) plus greenhouse gas emissions. USGS Benchmark Glacier Program since 1957.
