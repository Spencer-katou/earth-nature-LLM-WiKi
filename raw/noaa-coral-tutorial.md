# NOAA NOS — Corals tutorial (notes)

## What corals are
- Corals are cnidarians ("stinging animals"); jellyfish are relatives. A typical coral consists of hundreds of thousands of individual animals called polyps.
- Polyp: stomach opening at one end = mouth ringed with tentacles; food and waste use the same opening. Tentacles have nematocysts (stinging cells) for prey capture and defense.
- Most polyps feed at night: zooplankton to small fish; many also trap particles in mucus.
- Hard (stony) corals secrete rigid calcium carbonate (CaCO3) skeletons and are the primary reef builders. Soft corals lack rigid skeletons and do not build reefs; they have a flexible protein skeleton with spiny CaCO3 sclerites.
- ~800 stony coral species globally. Average stony polyp diameter 1–3 mm.
- Taxonomic class (Anthozoa) is NOT stated on these pages.

## Skeleton & growth
- The lower polyp secretes a cup (calyx): walls = theca, floor = basal plate. Periodically the polyp lifts and secretes a new basal plate, so the colony grows upward.
- Growth forms: branching, digitate, table, elkhorn, foliose, encrusting, massive (egg- to house-sized), mushroom.
- Growth rates: massive stony 0.3–2 cm/yr; branching stony up to 10 cm/yr; soft coral colonies 2–4 cm/yr.

## Zooxanthellae
- Photosynthetic cells living in tissues of most reef-building corals; mutualism. Coral supplies CO2 and water; algae supply oxygen, sugars, proteins, help remove waste.
- Up to 90% of organic material produced by zooxanthellae is transferred to the coral host.
- Give corals their colour. Stressed polyps expel them → white colony = bleaching; prolonged loss → starvation and death.
- Zooxanthellae are genetically diverse; some types tolerate heat better; corals may take up a different type after bleaching.
- Paradox: reefs need clear, nutrient-poor water yet are among the most productive and diverse marine environments.

## Growth conditions
- Shallow stony corals grow best at 23–29 °C; some tolerate up to 40 °C briefly.
- Most reef builders limited to depths where light reaches, ~30 m. Mesophotic ("middle light") corals live deeper with less light; understudied. Deep-sea corals live in dark water as cold as 4 °C and grow much more slowly.
- Salinity 32–42 parts per thousand. Need clear, low-turbidity water.

## Reef formation & types
- Reefs start when free-swimming larvae attach to hard surfaces at edges of islands/continents.
- Fringing reef: most common; grows seaward directly from shore.
- Barrier reef: borders shore at a distance, separated by a lagoon of open, often deep water.
- Atoll: fringing reef around a volcanic island that sinks below sea level while coral keeps growing upward; circular/oval with central lagoon.
- Reef formation can take up to 10,000 years; barrier reefs and atolls 100,000–30 million years.
- Zones seaward from shore: reef flat, reef crest/algal ridge, buttress zone, seaward slope.
- No specific named reefs on these pages.

## Distribution
- Most reef-building stony corals are in tropical/subtropical waters between 35°N and 35°S.
- Not stated: total reef area, share of marine species.
