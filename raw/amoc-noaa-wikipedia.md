# AMOC (notes)

## NOAA: global conveyor belt
- Starts in the Norwegian Sea: warm Gulf Stream water loses heat, cools, becomes denser and sinks; cold bottom water flows south along the western Atlantic.
- Recharged around Antarctica, splits into Indian and Pacific branches, warms, upwells, returns to the North Atlantic. One loop takes about 1,000 years; deep currents move a few cm per second. Carries more than 100× the Amazon's flow.

## Wikipedia: definition
- AMOC = main current system of the Atlantic, part of the global thermohaline circulation; northward warm salty upper flow, southward cold deep return flow. Makes up half of global thermohaline circulation (other half: Southern Ocean overturning).
- Gulf Stream as a whole is wind-driven; its northern extension (North Atlantic Current) gets much of its heat via the AMOC. AMOC carries up to 25% of heat toward the Northern Hemisphere; Atlantic is the only ocean with northward heat flow.
- North Atlantic Deep Water forms mainly in the Nordic Seas; Labrador Sea role contested. ~80% of NADW upwells in the Southern Ocean.

## Measurement & strength [S]
- Direct measurement since 2004 by the RAPID mooring array at 26°N. A 2025 multi-model analysis gave late-20th-century strength of ~14–26 Sv at 26°N.

## Weakening
- Climate change may be weakening the AMOC (ocean warming, Greenland meltwater); reconstructions suggest it was weaker in 2015 than pre-industrial; debate over climate change vs natural variability (verbatim).
- [S] Estimates: ~15% slowing (Caesar et al. 2021); 0.46 Sv/decade since 1950 (Pontes & Menviel 2024); a 2022 review: 5–25% since 1980, detection uncertain; some studies find no decline (Worthington et al. 2021; Volkov et al. 2024 on Florida Current). No consensus as of 2024.
- [S] IPCC AR6 (2021): very likely to decline in 21st century (high confidence); collapse not expected before 2100 (medium confidence).

## Tipping point [S]
- Armstrong McKay et al. 2022: collapse most likely triggered at ~4 °C warming (range 1.4–8 °C), unfolding over 15–300 years (most likely ~50).
- Ditlevsen & Ditlevsen 2023: most likely collapse ~2065 (95% range 2037–2109); many scientists sceptical.
- Baker et al. 2025 (Nature): 34 models resilient, 21st-century collapse unlikely. 2026 high-resolution work suggests collapse may be more likely than thought. Models disagree.

## Past changes (verbatim)
- Younger Dryas (12,800–11,700 years ago): abrupt AMOC slowdown from Laurentide meltwater (via Mackenzie River); NH temperatures returned to near-glacial levels, possibly within a decade.
- Dansgaard–Oeschger events and Heinrich events in the last glacial period linked to AMOC strengthening/weakening.
- Gulf Stream ~10% weaker c. 1200–1850, likely contributing to the Little Ice Age.

## Impacts of slowdown
- Cooler Europe (AMOC keeps NW Europe 4–10 °C warmer, depending on area); faster sea-level rise along North America; reduced North Atlantic primary production and weaker carbon sink; [S] southward shift of the tropical rain belt.
