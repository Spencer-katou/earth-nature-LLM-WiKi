# NASA — The Carbon Cycle (notes)

## Framing
- Carbon is the 4th most abundant element in the Universe. ~65,500 billion metric tons (Gt) of Earth's carbon is stored in rocks; the rest is in ocean, atmosphere, plants, soil, fossil fuels.
- Slow (geological, 100–200 million years) and fast (biological, lifespans) cycles. Acts as a thermostat over a few hundred thousand years. Contrast: Venus (CO2 in air, very hot) vs all carbon locked in rock (too cold).

## Slow carbon cycle
- 10^13–10^14 g (10–100 million metric tons) of carbon per year.
- Chemical weathering: rain + CO2 → carbonic acid dissolves rock; ions carried to the ocean.
- Carbonate deposition: Ca + bicarbonate → calcium carbonate, mostly by shell-builders (corals, coccolithophores, foraminifera) → limestone/marble.
- ~80% of carbon-containing rock is carbonate; ~20% is organic carbon in sediments (shale) → some becomes coal, oil, gas.
- Subduction + volcanoes return CO2. Volcanoes: 130–380 million metric tons CO2/yr. Humans: ~30 billion tons CO2/yr from fossil fuels, 100–300× volcanic.
- Feedback: more CO2 → warmer → more rain → more weathering → more deposition; rebalancing takes a few hundred thousand years.
- The ocean will absorb up to 85% of extra fossil carbon over millennia; slow, depends on surface-to-depth mixing.

## Fast carbon cycle
- 10^15–10^17 g (1,000–100,000 million metric tons) of carbon per year through the biosphere.
- Photosynthesis: CO2 + H2O + energy → CH2O + O2. Respiration/decay/fire: the reverse.
- Seasonal "breathing": Northern Hemisphere plants draw CO2 down in spring/summer; drawdown peaks in August at ~2 ppm (2010 data).

## Reservoirs / fluxes stated
- Rocks ~65,500 Gt C; Northern Hemisphere permafrost organic carbon 1,672 Pg; fossil fuels 2009 ~8.4 billion t C; land-use change just under 1 billion t C/yr.

## Ocean
- ~30% of human CO2 has entered the ocean. Surface pH dropped 0.1 since 1750 = ~30% increase in acidity. Fewer carbonate ions → thinner shells; acidic water dissolves shells. Warmer water may reduce phytoplankton.

## Land
- Land plants took up ~25% of human CO2. Doubling CO2 could raise modeled plant growth 12–76% if not limited by water/nutrients. Tropical deforestation ~12% of human CO2 emissions (as of 2008).
- Thawing 10% of Northern Hemisphere permafrost could add enough CO2 to raise temperatures 0.7°C (1.3°F) by 2100.

## Atmosphere & humans
- ~45% of emissions stay in the atmosphere; ~55% taken up by land + ocean ("about half" removed each year by the fast cycle).
- CO2 ~280 ppm pre-industrial → 387 ppm (article's data, ~2009), +39%, highest in ~2 million years.
- Methane 715 ppb (1750) → 1,774 ppb (2005), highest in ≥650,000 years.

## Past climate
- Milankovitch orbital cycles drove ice ages; CO2 drop amplified cooling. At the end of the last ice age, ~10,000 years ago, CO2 rose sharply.
- Antarctic ice cores: CO2 tracks temperature over the past 800,000 years until ~1900.
- Himalayan uplift starting 50 million years ago increased weathering.

## Climate effects
- Without greenhouse gases Earth's mean temperature would be −18°C (0°F); Venus-like runaway ~400°C.
- Greenhouse contributions: water vapor ~50%, clouds ~25%, CO2 ~20%, rest aerosols/minor gases. CO2 "sets the temperature" by controlling water vapor.
- Global temperature +0.8°C (1.4°F) since 1880; ≥0.6°C more already committed.

## Methods
- MODIS (net primary productivity, fires), Landsat (land cover), Mauna Loa CO2 record since 1957, EPICA Dome C ice cores (800,000 yr), deep-ocean oxygen isotopes, models.

## Open questions
- Will plants keep absorbing? How much permafrost carbon? Will ocean uptake slow? How much acidification?
