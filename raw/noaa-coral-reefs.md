# NOAA — Coral reef ecosystems (notes)

## Corals
- Coral polyps are the main reef builders; forms include large colonies, fans, small solitary organisms. Thousands of species; some in warm shallow tropical seas, others in cold dark deep water.
- Shallow reef-building corals host photosynthetic algae, **zooxanthellae** (mutualism): coral gives shelter and compounds; algae give carbohydrates and oxygen and help remove waste.
- Deep-sea corals lack zooxanthellae; feed on plankton and organic matter. Deep reefs support a wide array of life in a comparatively barren world.

## Biodiversity
- "Rainforests of the sea": complex habitat for shelter, feeding, breeding.
- ~25% of the ocean's fish depend on healthy reefs.
- Northwest Hawaiian Islands (Papahānaumokuākea National Marine Monument): >7,000 species of fishes, invertebrates, plants, sea turtles, birds, marine mammals.
- Other places named: National Marine Sanctuary of American Samoa (only true tropical reef in the US sanctuary system), Great Barrier Reef, Florida Keys, Flower Garden Banks.

## Value
- Coastal protection from storms/erosion, jobs, recreation, food, new medicines; more than half a billion people depend on reefs. Fishing/diving add hundreds of millions of dollars locally; net global value "tens of billions" USD/yr (exact figure not confirmed). Culturally important to Indigenous peoples.
- Reef structures take decades or longer to build.

## Threats
- Natural: disease, predators, storms. Human: pollution, sedimentation, unsustainable fishing, climate change (warming, ocean acidification).
- Bleaching can lead to death; corals can recover if conditions improve in time, but full ecosystem recovery takes many years.
- 2014–2017 global bleaching event, partly linked to a strong El Niño, affected 70% of reef ecosystems worldwide; the Great Barrier Reef was hard hit ("hundreds of miles" bleached).
- Restoration: nursery-grown corals transplanted to damaged areas.
