# NASA / NOAA — Global sea level (notes)

## Rise
- NASA: satellite altimetry shows global mean sea level up 115.3 mm since 1993 (chart covers 1993–2024; no glacial isostatic adjustment applied).
- NOAA: in 2014 global sea level was 67 mm (2.6 in) above the 1993 average — then the highest annual average in the satellite record.
- Total rise since 1880: not stated.

## Rate
- NASA: annual rate rose from 2.0 mm/yr (0.08 in/yr) in 1993 to a current 4.4 mm/yr (0.17 in/yr); year of "current" not stated.
- NOAA: rising about 3.2 mm (1/8 in) per year, at an increasing rate (no year given).

## Causes
- Two main causes: thermal expansion of warming seawater, and added water from melting land ice (glaciers, ice sheets). No percentage shares given.
- NOAA: the ocean absorbs more than 90% of the extra heat from human emissions.

## Projections & impacts
- NOAA: with continued warming, sea level will likely rise for many centuries at rates higher than this century. No numeric 2050/2100 projections.
- NOAA: US nuisance (high-tide) flooding is 300–900% more frequent than 50 years ago; almost 40% of US population lives in high-density coastal areas.
- Local sea level differs from global due to subsidence, groundwater withdrawal, currents, glacial isostatic rebound.

## Measurement
- Tide stations measure local relative sea level; satellite altimeters measure average height of the whole ocean.
- NASA missions: TOPEX/Poseidon, Jason-1, -2, -3, Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich.
