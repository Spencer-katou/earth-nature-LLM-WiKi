# NOAA — The water cycle (notes)

## Concept
- Continuous movement of water within Earth and atmosphere, changing between liquid, solid, vapor. Humans play a major role in the modern cycle.
- Two core concepts: **pools** (where water is stored: lakes, atmospheric vapor, glacier ice) and **fluxes** (how water moves between pools, incl. state changes).
- Often taught as a simple evaporation–condensation–precipitation circle; reality is much more complicated; paths through ecosystems are not completely understood.

## Pools
- Salt water: heading says 97.5% of Earth's water; body says 97% stored in ocean; pie chart: oceans 96.5%, other saline water 0.9%, freshwater 2.5%.
- Freshwater = 2.5% of Earth's water. Of freshwater: glaciers, ice sheets, permanent snow ~68% (chart 68.7%); groundwater ~30% (chart 30.1%); permafrost & ground ice <1%; lakes, rivers, wetlands ~0.25% (most important for human use); surface & other freshwater 1.2% (chart).
- Breakdown of "surface and other freshwater": ground ice & permafrost 69.0%, lakes 20.9%, soil moisture 3.8%, atmosphere 3.0%, swamps & marshes 2.6%, rivers 0.49%, living things 0.26%.

## Fluxes
- Evaporation: liquid → vapor, absorbing energy (sun, atmosphere, body heat).
- Transpiration: evaporation from plant leaves; much water taken up by roots leaves this way.
- Condensation: vapor → droplets (clouds, dew) when air holds more vapor than it can at the current temperature.
- Sublimation: snow/ice → vapor directly. Deposition: vapor → ice directly (e.g., hoar frost).
- Precipitation: rain, snow, etc.
- Runoff & streamflow: over land, through lakes/streams, as glacier ice, over impermeable surfaces.
- Infiltration: surface water into soil. Percolation: further down into groundwater.
- Transportation: through the atmosphere as liquid/ice/vapor; atmospheric rivers are a concentrated form.

## Monitoring
- Satellites, rain gauges, stream gauges; NOAA forecasts precipitation, fog, evapotranspiration, runoff, river levels, droughts, floods. CoCoRaHS volunteer precipitation network.

## Humans
- Uses: drinking, hygiene, food, industry, hydropower, recreation. Dams and river diversion change storage; water quality limits availability.
- Some sources (e.g., groundwater) replenish slowly; using freshwater faster than it is restored depletes supply.

## Climate change
- Warming changes where/when/how much water is available; glacier and sea ice melt moves water into the ocean.
- Warmer air → more evaporation and holds more vapor → longer gaps between rains and more intense rain; both drought and heavy precipitation; more moisture can make hurricanes bigger/stronger; longer dry spells → drought, wildfire.
- Evaporation/condensation absorb/release heat → affects weather and Earth's energy balance.
- Water vapor traps energy; warming increases evaporation → positive feedback loop.
