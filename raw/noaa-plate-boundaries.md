# NOAA — Plate boundaries & tectonic shift (notes)

## Lithosphere & plates
- Ocean Exploration: the lithosphere includes the crust and upper mantle; it is broken into tectonic plates that move slowly.
- NOS: "Earth's crust, called the lithosphere" consists of 15–20 moving plates; a photo caption says roughly a dozen major plates plus several minor plates.
- Plates rest on the hot, molten rock of the mantle. Heat from radioactive processes in Earth's interior drives the motion.

## Speeds
- Average ~1.5 cm (0.6 in) per year. Coastal California almost 5 cm (2 in) per year relative to the stable continental US interior.

## Boundary types
- Divergent: plates move apart; magma rises to form new oceanic crust; earthquakes common. Example: Mid-Atlantic Ridge.
- Convergent: plates collide; edges buckle into mountains or one plate bends down into a deep trench. Oceanic vs continental: oceanic crust is forced down and melts; magma rises forming volcano chains parallel to the boundary; powerful earthquakes. Example: Pacific Ring of Fire.
- Transform: plates slide past each other; crust is cracked but neither created nor destroyed; offsets features, makes linear fault valleys / undersea canyons; earthquakes common. Example: San Andreas fault zone (extends underwater).

## Other
- Grinding at plate seams causes earthquakes and eruptions; California's frequent earthquakes are linked to fast motion.
- ~250 million years ago land formed the supercontinent Pangaea surrounded by a single ocean, Panthalassa; it broke apart; South America's east coast fits Africa's west coast.
- Plate motion is measured by geodesy (NOAA National Geodetic Survey).
- Not covered: layer depths/temperatures, hotspots, Wegener/seafloor-spreading history.
